Scene 01 · Take 6
Six years in SME banking

For six years, my desk has faced small businesses.

Traders, tailors, caterers, makers. Founders who keep the books in a notebook and the whole plan in their head. My work is to design banking around how they really live and work, not around a form.

I lead a national platform for small and medium enterprises: product strategy, partnerships and the programmes that bring founders back every week. I led the design and launch of a women-focused banking proposition, and I shaped a concept that lets customers finance solar power through a marketplace of trusted vendors.

The lesson I carry everywhere: the hard part is rarely the product. It is seeing the customer clearly enough to build the right one.

Romance · Platform
Vows.ng

One couple. Several ceremonies. A guest list that will not stop growing. Free wedding websites with invitation codes, RSVP for every ceremony, seating, aso-ebi, gifts by bank transfer and check-in at the gate.

The problem
Nigerian weddings have several ceremonies, long guest lists, aso-ebi and gifts by bank transfer. Most wedding websites were built for one ceremony and a registry.
The hardest decision
Guests had to RSVP without signing in, yet never be able to read the guest list. The database cannot grant a write to a record you cannot read, so I designed a write-only drop box: guests post a code, a function checks it and files the answer.
Next time
Plan for mobile data from day one. The first prototype carried about 1.6 MB of artwork inside the page, and a guest on a phone at the venue is the case that matters.

Sci-fi · Desktop AI
AI Creation Studio

A film studio that lives on a laptop. Write a script and it returns an animated episode with voices and music, plus carousels and reels for social media. Local AI, no cloud bill.

The problem
Making animated stories and social content usually means several paid tools and a cloud subscription for each one.
The hardest decision
Run everything locally: the language model, the voices and the video engine all live on the laptop. It is slower than the cloud, but it costs nothing per episode and nothing leaves the machine.
Next time
Lock the script format earlier. The story engine went through twelve releases, and the features were easier to add once the format stopped moving.

Thriller · Browser game
Shadow Vaults

A browser game designed and coded from scratch. Concept, mechanics and code, end to end. It plays right in your browser.

The problem
I wanted a real game that anyone could play from a link, on a laptop or a phone, with nothing to install.
The hardest decision
One self-contained file with no libraries. Procedural floors, a boss, a shop, saved progress and touch controls all had to fit inside it, plus an Android build.
Next time
Capture real gameplay from the first day. The first release went out with placeholder screenshots.

Action · Health app
Fit & Fed

Moving and eating well, turned into a daily streak of XP and challenges. A web app with an Android build.

The problem
Staying consistent is the hard part of getting healthy, and eating well can feel expensive.
The hardest decision
No ads and no trackers. It works fully offline, the data stays on the phone, and sign-in is optional. Meal plans start from the weekly food budget the user types in.
Next time
Launch smaller. Nine routines and a 27-move library is a lot to put in front of a first-time user.

Family · Comic book
Ada’s Big Space Adventure

A young hero crosses the stars to find a friend. An original picture book, written and illustrated for readers aged five to eight.

The problem
Young readers deserve adventure stories where a child who looks like them is the hero.
The hardest decision
Write it as a series, not a single book, so Ada can return for more adventures in the same visual style.
Next time
Test the pages with children before the final illustrations, not after.

Adventure · Illustrated serial
The Lost Current

Under the sea, a reef city is losing the current that keeps it alive, and three young heroes go looking for it. A bedtime serial for ages nine to twelve, one watercolour illustration per episode.

The problem
Bedtime stories for older children often have a moral but no real stakes. Readers aged nine to twelve want both.
The hardest decision
Keep a story bible that tracks every character, secret and change, so each 800-word episode stays consistent across the whole serial.
Next time
Decide the ending before writing episode one.

Community · Web app
FreeCycle

Neighbours give away what they no longer need, and someone nearby gives it a second life. Less waste, more community.

The problem
Two everyday problems: we throw away usable things because giving them away is a hassle, and good cooked food goes to waste.
The hardest decision
Turn both into a 30-second post. A neighbour claims it, a volunteer rider collects it, and everyone earns karma for keeping things out of the bin.
Next time
Start with one estate or one street, so the map has enough people on it to feel alive.

Adventure · EdTech
StudyQuest

Studying, turned into quests, XP and rewards, so students keep coming back for one more chapter.

The problem
Secondary-school students preparing for exams lose motivation long before the exam date.
The hardest decision
Build it as an installable web app, so it works offline and needs no app store. Inside: quests, boss battles, streaks and spaced repetition.
Next time
Pick the final name earlier. It started as StudyQuest and grew into Beliyona Learn.
